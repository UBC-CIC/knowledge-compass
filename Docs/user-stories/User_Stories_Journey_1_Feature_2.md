## USER STORY 121: As a parent, I want the system to recognize and clearly explain my situation
(e.g., partial day attendance) so I understand what is happening.
### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent has been told their child can only attend school for part of the day, but they are unfamiliar with
the term "partial day attendance" and are unsure whether what is happening is normal, appropriate,
temporary, or something they should be concerned about.
### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
#### Acceptance Criteria
The system should:
explain partial day attendance in plain, parent-friendly language
distinguish between voluntary and involuntary partial day attendance
provide examples of what partial day attendance may look like in practice
explain common reasons schools may implement partial day attendance
help parents understand how partial day attendance can affect a child's education and
participation
explain why understanding the concept is important when discussing supports and
accommodations
avoid educational jargon wherever possible
provide links or references to trusted supporting resources
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
#### User Flow/ Conversation
**Parent:**
The school says my daughter can only attend mornings. Is this partial day attendance?
**System:**
1. Define the concept.
"Partial day attendance occurs when a student attends school for less than the full school day."
2. Clarify the context.
"Sometimes reduced attendance is requested by families. In other situations, the school initiates
the reduced schedule."
3. Explain the situation.
"When the school limits attendance because it believes it cannot currently support full
participation, this is often referred to as involuntary partial day attendance."
4. Provide examples.
"Examples may include:
attending mornings only
attending afternoons only
attending certain days of the week
being sent home regularly before the end of the day"
5. Explain why it matters.
"Understanding partial day attendance can help you ask informed questions about supports,
accommodations, and plans for returning to full-time participation."
6. Reinforce learning.
"Knowing what the situation is called can make it easier to understand your options and
advocate effectively."
7. Suggest next step.
"Let's explore why your child's attendance was reduced and what information may help you
move forward."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
#### Content Required
The system needs:
definition of partial day attendance
definition of involuntary partial day attendance
distinction between voluntary and involuntary reduced schedules
common attendance reduction scenarios
examples from elementary, middle, and high school settings
impacts of reduced attendance on learning, participation, and social inclusion
plain-language explanations suitable for families
trusted inclusive education resources and references
common terminology used by schools and education systems
### Notes
#### Estimated Complexity
Small (1 day)
This is primarily an educational story.
The focus is on understanding a key concept rather than navigating a process.
#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify that the system:
provides a clear definition of partial day attendance
explains involuntary partial day attendance
distinguishes between family-requested and school-imposed reductions
provides understandable examples
avoids technical or policy-heavy language
helps parents understand why the concept matters
The system should consistently help parents answer:
"What does partial day attendance mean?"
before moving into more advanced discussions about rights, advocacy, and escalation.

## USER STORY 122: As a parent, I want to know what informal partial day attendance looks like
so that I can identify what's happening to my child.
### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent notices that their child is attending school less than their peers or is regularly being sent home
early, but nobody has used the term "partial day attendance." The parent is unsure whether this is a
temporary accommodation, a common practice, or something they should be paying closer attention to.
### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
#### Acceptance Criteria
The system should:
explain that partial day attendance is not always formally identified or documented
provide examples of situations that may indicate informal partial day attendance
help parents recognize patterns of reduced participation
distinguish between occasional events and ongoing attendance reductions
explain common language schools may use instead of the term "partial day attendance"
* help parents identify when a reduced schedule may be becoming normalized
encourage parents to seek clarification when attendance expectations are unclear
avoid assuming all reduced attendance situations are inappropriate
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
#### User Flow/ Conversation
**Parent:**
The school never said my son is on partial day attendance, but he only goes in the mornings now.
**System:**
1. Acknowledge the concern.
"Sometimes partial day attendance is not formally identified, even though a student is attending
less than the full school day."
2. Explain informal situations.
"Reduced attendance can occur through informal arrangements or practices that develop over
time."
3. Provide examples.
"Examples may include:
* attending only mornings or afternoons
 regularly being picked up early
* being asked to stay home on certain days
shortened schedules described as temporary but continuing for extended
periods
missing activities that peers are expected to attend"
4. Highlight common language.
"Schools may describe these arrangements as temporary, gradual, flexible, or supportive rather
than using the term 'partial day attendance."
5. Encourage reflection.
"A useful question is whether your child is participating in the same school day and activities as
their peers."
6. Suggest clarification.
"If attendance expectations are unclear, you may wish to ask the school how the current
arrangement is being documented and reviewed."
7. Recommend next step.
"Let's look at your child's current attendance pattern and determine whether it may fit a partial
day attendance situation."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
#### Content Required
The system needs:
* examples of informal partial day attendance arrangements
common attendance reduction patterns
examples of language schools may use instead of "partial day attendance"
indicators that a temporary arrangement may be becoming long-term
differences between occasional accommodations and ongoing reduced schedules
examples of participation limitations beyond attendance
guidance on seeking clarification and documentation
plain-language explanations suitable for families
### Notes
#### Estimated Complexity
Small (1 day)
This is primarily an awareness and recognition story.
The goal is helping parents recognize patterns rather than teaching a process.
#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify the system appropriately recognizes scenarios such as:
* mornings only attendance
afternoons only attendance
repeated early pickups
reduced attendance on specific days
* field-trip exclusions
temporary arrangements that continue indefinitely
attendance restrictions that are not formally documented
The system should consistently:
help parents identify patterns
explain informal partial day attendance
encourage clarification
avoid jumping to conclusions
help parents determine whether further questions are warranted

schedule. The arrangement appears to be planned and intentional, but the parent is unsure what
formalized partial day attendance looks like, how it is typically documented, and what information they
should expect to receive.
### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
#### Acceptance Criteria
The system should:
explain what formalized partial day attendance is
describe common indicators that a reduced schedule has become formalized
help parents understand how such arrangements may be communicated or documented
explain the types of information parents may expect to receive
distinguish formalized arrangements from informal attendance reductions
help parents identify whether review dates, goals, or return-to-full-time plans exist
explain why documentation and clarity are important
encourage parents to seek clarification when arrangements are unclear
avoid implying that all formalized arrangements are inappropriate
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
User Flow / Conversation
**Parent:**
The school says my daughter will only attend mornings for the foreseeable future and they've put it in
writing. Is that considered partial day attendance?
**System:**
1. Acknowledge the situation.
"It sounds like the school has implemented a planned reduced schedule and communicated that
decision formally."
2. Define the concept.
"Formalized partial day attendance generally occurs when a student's reduced schedule has
been intentionally established and documented by the school."
3. Describe common indicators.
"Examples may include:
* * a written attendance plan
documented meeting decisions
scheduled review dates
communication outlining attendance expectations
documented conditions for returning to full-time attendance"
4. Explain why it matters.
"When a reduced schedule becomes formalized, it is important that parents understand the
reasons, expectations, and plans associated with it."
5. Encourage review.
"You may wish to ask:
* Why was this decision made?
* How will progress be monitored?
When will the arrangement be reviewed?
What steps support a return to full-time attendance?"
6. Highlight clarity.
"A formal arrangement should generally be clear enough that everyone understands what is
expected and how future decisions will be made."
7. Suggest next step.
"Let's review the information you've received and identify any gaps or unanswered questions."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
#### Content Required
The system needs:
* definition of formalized partial day attendance
examples of documented attendance arrangements
examples of attendance plans and meeting outcomes
common elements found in formal attendance decisions
review and monitoring practices
examples of return-to-full-time planning
differences between formal and informal arrangements
guidance on clarification and documentation requests
plain-language explanations suitable for families

#### Estimated Complexity
Small (1 day)
This is primarily an educational story focused on helping parents recognize and understand formal
attendance arrangements.
#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify the system appropriately identifies situations involving:
written attendance plans
documented reduced schedules
meeting decisions
review timelines
attendance agreements
written communications about reduced participation
The system should consistently:
explain what makes an arrangement formalized
distinguish formal and informal situations
encourage understanding and clarification
highlight the importance of review and documentation
help parents identify missing information

## USER STORY 125: As a parent, I want to know the consequences of informal PDA so that my

### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent notices that their child is frequently missing parts of the school day or school experience, but

is having a broader impact on their child's learning, development, participation, or support planning.

The system should:
explain how informal partial day attendance can affect a student's educational experience
describe how reduced participation may limit opportunities to observe and understand support
needs
* explain potential impacts on academic progress
explain potential impacts on social participation and belonging
* help parents understand how support plans are often informed by participation and observation
provide examples of how unmet support needs may go unnoticed when participation is reduced
explain that impacts may vary from student to student
avoid assuming negative outcomes in every situation
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?

**Parent:**
My son only attends mornings and often misses activities in the afternoon. Could that be affecting the
support he receives?
**System:**
1. Acknowledge the concern.
"That's an important question. Reduced participation can sometimes affect how a student's
strengths, challenges, and support needs are understood."
2. Explain the connection.
"When students are not fully participating in the school day, staff may have fewer opportunities
to observe how supports are working in different situations."
3. Provide examples.
"For example:
* support needs may not be fully identified
accommodations may not be adjusted appropriately
social challenges may be missed
academic gaps may become harder to recognize"
4. Explain the broader impact.
"Over time, reduced participation can limit opportunities for learning, relationship building, and
skill development."
5. Encourage reflection.
"You may wish to consider whether there are parts of the school day where important
information about your child's needs is not being captured."
6. Reinforce purpose.
"Understanding these impacts can help families advocate for meaningful participation and
appropriate supports."
7. Suggest next step.
"Let's look at which parts of your child's school experience are being missed and what
information may be affected."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
#### Content Required
The system needs:
impacts of informal partial day attendance
* examples of participation-related support identification
support planning and accommodation processes
examples of missed opportunities for observation and assessment
academic impacts of reduced participation
social and emotional impacts of exclusion
examples of how support needs are identified in school settings
inclusive education principles related to participation
plain-language explanations suitable for families
### Notes
#### Estimated Complexity
Small (1 day)
This is primarily an educational story focused on helping parents understand why participation matters.
The story is less about attendance itself and more about the downstream impact on support planning
and student outcomes.
#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify that the system:
explains how reduced participation can affect support identification
provides examples of missed opportunities for observation and assessment
connects participation to support planning
explains academic, social, and developmental impacts
avoids alarmist language
encourages informed advocacy

"What might my child be missing if this continues?"

## USER STORY 12A: As a parent, I want to know why inclusive ed matters for my child so that I
can be more informed.
### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent understands the concept of inclusive education and has heard about its benefits, but is unsure
how it applies to their own child. They want to understand why inclusion matters in the context of their
child's education, relationships, future opportunities, and overall quality of life.
### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
#### Acceptance Criteria
The system should:
help parents connect inclusive education concepts to their child's daily experiences
explain how inclusion can affect learning, participation, belonging, and relationships
describe how inclusive experiences can influence future opportunities
provide examples that are relevant to different ages and educational stages
help parents understand the long-term significance of educational decisions
encourage parents to consider both current and future impacts
present information in plain language
avoid assuming that every child's needs or circumstances are identical
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
#### User Flow/ Conversation
**Parent:**
I understand what inclusive education is, but why does it matter specifically for my child?
**System:**
1. Acknowledge the question.
"That's an important question. Understanding why inclusion matters can help you make
informed decisions about your child's education and future."
2. Connect to everyday experiences.
"Inclusive education influences more than academics. It affects who your child learns with, who
they build friendships with, and how they participate in school life."
3. Explain participation.
"When students participate alongside their peers, they have more opportunities to learn,
contribute, develop relationships, and build confidence."
4. Explain future impact.
"Experiences during school often shape future opportunities in employment, community
involvement, post-secondary education, and independent living."
5. Personalize the concept.
"The specific benefits may look different for every child, but meaningful participation and
belonging are important considerations for all students."
6. Reinforce reflection.
"When educational decisions arise, it can be helpful to ask how each option supports your
child's long-term participation and inclusion."
7. Suggest next step.
"Let's talk about your child's current situation and explore how inclusion may apply to them."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
#### Content Required
The system needs:
principles of inclusive education
participation and belonging concepts
age-appropriate examples of inclusion
academic, social, and developmental impacts of inclusion
long-term outcomes associated with participation
examples of inclusive educational experiences
transition and future-planning considerations
plain-language explanations suitable for families
evidence-informed descriptions of inclusive practices
### Notes
#### Estimated Complexity
Small (1 day)
This is primarily an educational and reflective story.
The emphasis is on helping parents connect broad concepts to their own child's experience.
#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify the system:
personalizes the discussion to the child's situation
explains practical impacts of inclusion
connects current educational experiences to future outcomes
provides understandable examples
avoids generic or overly academic explanations
reinforces participation and belonging
The system should consistently help parents answer:
"Why does inclusion matter for my child?"
rather than:
"Why do educators talk about inclusion?"

## USER STORY 12B: As a parent, I want to know who I can contact for help/support for inclusive
ed so that I won't have to do this alone.
### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent wants to better understand and advocate for inclusive education but is unsure where to find
guidance, expertise, or support. They know they cannot be expected to navigate educational systems,
policies, and advocacy efforts entirely on their own.
### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
#### Acceptance Criteria
The system should:
explain that support is available to families navigating inclusive education
identify common sources of support and guidance
* explain the different roles various support organizations and individuals may play
* help parents understand when different supports may be useful
distinguish between educational, advocacy, community, and peer supports
encourage parents to seek support proactively rather than waiting for a crisis
* provide information in plain language
help parents understand that building a support network is a normal part of advocacy
avoid assuming a single support source will meet every family's needs
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
User Flow / Conversation
**Parent:**
I believe in inclusive education, but I honestly don't know who can help me navigate all of this.
**System:**
1. Acknowledge the concern.
"Many families feel that way. Inclusive education can involve policies, planning, meetings,
supports, and decisions that can be difficult to navigate alone."
2. Normalize seeking support.
"It's completely reasonable to seek guidance and support from others."
3. Explain support options.
"Support may come from:
* parent advocacy organizations
* disability organizations
 community support groups
* 4. Explain roles.
educational support services
other families with lived experience
professionals who understand inclusive education"
"Different supports can help in different ways. Some provide information, some offer advocacy
guidance, and others provide emotional or peer support."
5. Encourage connection.
"Many families find it helpful to connect with people who have experienced similar situations."
6. Build confidence.
"You don't need to become an expert overnight. Having the right supports can make the
journey much easier."
7. Suggest next step.
"Let's explore what type of support would be most helpful for your situation right now."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
#### Content Required
The system needs:
inclusive education support organizations
parent advocacy organizations
disability support organizations
community-based support services
peer support networks
educational support resources
examples of support roles and services
referral and navigation resources
plain-language descriptions of support options
jurisdiction-specific resources where available
### Notes
#### Estimated Complexity
Small (1 day)
This is primarily a resource navigation and support awareness story.
The main value comes from helping parents understand that support exists and where it may be found.
#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify the system:
identifies multiple types of support
explains how different supports can help
* avoids assuming one organization solves every problem
encourages proactive help-seeking
* provides understandable explanations
reinforces that parents do not have to navigate the journey alone
The system should consistently help parents answer:
"Who can help me navigate inclusive education?"
rather than:
"Where do I file a complaint?"

## USER STORY 12C: As a parent, I want to know how to be involved to change PDA in
Canada so that others can have better access to inclusive ed.
### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent has gained experience navigating partial day attendance and inclusive education challenges
and wants to understand how they can help improve the system for other families. They are interested in
contributing their voice, experience, or expertise but are unsure where to start.
### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
#### Acceptance Criteria
The system should:
explain ways families can contribute to improving inclusive education
identify opportunities for participation at local, regional, and national levels
provide examples of advocacy and engagement activities
explain how lived experience can influence awareness, policy, and practice
identify organizations and groups working in inclusive education
encourage participation at a level that feels comfortable for the parent
explain that meaningful contributions can take many forms
avoid promoting specific political positions or campaigns
focus on constructive involvement and system improvement
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
User Flow / Conversation
**Parent:**
We've been through this ourselves. Is there anything I can do to help other families?
**System:**
1. Acknowledge the motivation.
"Many parents who have navigated these challenges want to help create a better experience for
future families."
2. Explain the value of lived experience.
"Your experiences can provide valuable insight into what works, what doesn't, and where
improvements may be needed."
3. Provide examples of involvement.
"Families contribute in many ways, including:
* participating in consultations
sharing their experiences
 joining advocacy or advisory groups
 supporting other families
participating in research or surveys
helping raise awareness"
4. Normalize different levels of involvement.
"Not everyone wants to become an advocate. Even small contributions can make a meaningful
difference."
5. Connect to the bigger picture.
"Many improvements in inclusive education have been influenced by families sharing their
experiences and perspectives."
6. Encourage reflection.
"Think about the type of involvement that feels realistic and meaningful for you."
7. Suggest next step.
"Let's explore organizations or opportunities that align with your interests."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
#### Content Required
The system needs:
inclusive education organizations and networks
advocacy and engagement opportunities
parent advisory groups
consultation and participation opportunities
examples of family-led system improvement efforts
peer support opportunities
research participation opportunities
awareness and education initiatives
plain-language descriptions of involvement options
### Notes
#### Estimated Complexity
Small (1 day)
This is primarily a resource navigation and awareness story.
The goal is to help interested parents move from individual advocacy to broader contribution.
#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify the system:
* identifies multiple forms of involvement
explains participation opportunities in plain language
encourages constructive engagement
avoids political advocacy
respects different levels of commitment
recognizes the value of lived experience
The system should consistently help parents answer:
"How can I help improve things for other families?"
rather than:
"How do I fix the entire system?"
