# USER JOURNEY 2: Transitions to Public School and Community

Support families in developing a clear, flexible vision for their child's future without prematurely narrowing options based on assumptions or system pressures.

## FEATURE 1: Help families plan for their child's future without limiting possibilities.

Give families the confidence to keep doors open and make decisions aligned with their child's potential not system constraints.

## USER STORY 211: As a parent, I want to know what courses my child should be taking so that my child has more choice/options for the future.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is approaching a transition point where course selections and educational pathway decisions are being discussed. The parent wants to ensure these decisions keep future opportunities open and are based on the student's interests, strengths, and goals rather than assumptions about disability or support needs.
(Note: This is about "Don't make today's decisions based solely on today's challenges".)

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?

#### Acceptance Criteria
The system should:
* explain how course selections can influence future educational and career opportunities
* help parents understand common educational pathways and graduation requirements
* encourage planning based on the student's interests, strengths, and goals
* explain the potential consequences of narrowing options too early
* help parents understand how accommodations and supports can enable participation in a wider range of courses
* provide examples of questions families can ask during course-planning discussions
* explain that pathways can change over time and should be reviewed regularly
* encourage keeping options open whenever appropriate
* avoid recommending specific courses without sufficient context

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?

#### User Flow/ Conversation

**Parent:**
The school is asking us to choose courses for next year. How do I know which ones will keep options open for my son?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the decision.**
   "Course selection can feel overwhelming because the choices made today may influence future opportunities."
2. **Reframe the discussion.**
   "Rather than asking which courses are easiest, it can be helpful to consider which options keep the greatest number of future pathways available."
3. **Explore goals.**
   "What interests, strengths, or future possibilities has your child expressed?"
4. **Explain the impact.**
   "Some courses serve as prerequisites for future programs, training, or post-secondary opportunities. Understanding those connections can help families make informed choices."
5. **Discuss supports.**
   "A student's support needs do not automatically determine which courses they can pursue. Accommodations and supports may help students participate successfully in a wide range of courses."
6. **Encourage future-focused planning.**
   "When possible, many families choose pathways that preserve flexibility while interests and goals continue to develop."
7. **Suggest next step.**
   "Let's look at your child's interests and explore which course options may help keep future opportunities open."

### 4. What information does it need to know?

#### Content Required
The system needs:
* graduation pathways and requirements
* course prerequisite structures
* post-secondary entrance requirements
* employment and career pathway examples
* transition planning concepts
* inclusive education principles
* accommodation and support planning information
* examples of course-planning discussions
* common misconceptions about educational pathways
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

#### Notes
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small-Medium (1-2 days)
* The functionality is primarily educational and planning-focused.
* The challenge is helping families think long-term without positioning the system as a guidance counselor.

#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify the system:
* explains course-planning concepts clearly
* connects course decisions to future opportunities
* encourages student-centered planning
* promotes keeping options open where possible
* recognizes the role of accommodations and supports
* avoids making definitive academic recommendations
* encourages ongoing review and adjustment

The system should consistently help parents answer:
"How can I keep future opportunities available for my child?"
rather than:
"What course should my child take next semester?"

## USER STORY 212: As a parent, I want to make sure my child is getting credits so they can graduate.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is progressing through school and participating in courses or programs, but the parent is unsure whether their child is earning the credits needed to graduate. They want to understand how graduation requirements work and whether current educational decisions could affect future graduation opportunities.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?

#### Acceptance Criteria
The system should:
* explain how credits contribute to graduation requirements
* help parents understand the difference between courses that earn credits and those that may not
* explain common graduation pathways
* help parents identify questions they can ask about their child's progress toward graduation
* explain how modifications, alternate programs, or pathway changes may affect credit accumulation
* encourage regular review of graduation progress
* help parents understand how course selection decisions connect to graduation goals
* avoid making graduation determinations without sufficient information

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?

#### User Flow/ Conversation

**Parent:**
My daughter is doing well in school, but I'm not sure if the courses she's taking count toward graduation.

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.**
   "Many parents are surprised to learn that not all educational pathways lead to the same graduation outcomes."
2. **Explain the concept.**
   "Graduation requirements are typically based on earning specific credits and completing required courses."
3. **Explain why it matters.**
   "Understanding how credits are earned can help families ensure that future options remain available."
4. **Encourage clarification.**
   "You may wish to ask:
   * Is my child earning graduation credits?
   * Which graduation pathway are they currently following?
   * Are there any courses or requirements we should be aware of?
   * How will current decisions affect future graduation options?"
5. **Connect to planning.**
   "Regularly reviewing graduation progress can help identify concerns early, before important opportunities are lost."
6. **Reinforce the bigger picture.**
   "Graduation planning is not just about finishing school. It can also influence access to future education, training, employment, and community opportunities."
7. **Suggest next step.**
   "Let's explore your child's current courses and what graduation requirements may apply."

### 4. What information does it need to know?

#### Content Required
The system needs:
* graduation requirements and pathways
* credit accumulation concepts
* required and elective course structures
* modified and alternate program information
* course-to-credit relationships
* transition planning concepts
* post-secondary and employment pathway considerations
* questions families can ask about graduation planning
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

#### Notes
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small (1 day)
* This is primarily an educational and planning story.
* Most of the value comes from helping families understand graduation requirements before important decisions are made.

#### Testing Considerations
Testers should verify the system:
* explains credits and graduation requirements clearly
* connects credits to future opportunities
* encourages proactive graduation planning
* identifies useful questions for families
* avoids making assumptions about a student's graduation status
* helps parents understand the impact of educational pathway decisions

The system should consistently help parents answer:
"Is my child on track to graduate and keep future options available?"
rather than:
"Will my child graduate?"

## User Story 215: As a parent, I want to make sure my child is succeeding in the prerequisites so that they can take additional courses in the following years.

* help parents understand indicators that a student may be struggling
* encourage regular monitoring of progress rather than waiting until course selection time
* identify questions parents can ask about academic progress and supports
* explain how accommodations and supports may help students succeed
* help parents understand the potential impact of failing or withdrawing from prerequisite courses
* encourage early intervention when concerns arise
* focus on planning and support rather than blame

My son wants to take advanced courses later, but I'm worried he's struggling in the prerequisite course this year.

2. **Explain the connection.**
   "Many future courses, programs, and opportunities depend on successfully completing prerequisite requirements."
3. **Explore current progress.**
   "What feedback have you received about your child's progress so far?"
4. **Identify warning signs.**
   "Possible indicators that additional support may be needed include:
   * declining grades
   * missed assignments
   * difficulty understanding course material
   * increased frustration or disengagement

5. **Suggest questions.**
   "You may wish to ask:
   * Is my child on track to meet course expectations?
   * What supports are available?
   * Are accommodations being implemented effectively?
   * What can we do now to improve success?"
6. **Reinforce proactive planning.**
   "Addressing concerns early often provides more options than waiting until the end of the course."
7. **Suggest next step.**

#### Content Required

#### Testing Considerations

* examples of successful alternative pathways
* plain-language explanations suitable for families
