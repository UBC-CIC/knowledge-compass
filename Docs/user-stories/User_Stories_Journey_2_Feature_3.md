## USER STORY 231: As a parent, I want to understand what I need to advocate for at each stage so that my child is on an inclusive ed pathway.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is approaching a transition to a new grade, school, or stage of education. The parent knows important decisions will be made during the transition process but is unsure what issues they should be paying attention to, what decisions may affect future opportunities, and how to advocate effectively at each stage.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain the purpose of transition planning
* help parents understand that different advocacy priorities emerge at different stages of education
* identify common transition points (elementary, middle school, high school, post-secondary, community)
* explain key decisions commonly made at each stage
* help parents understand how those decisions can affect future opportunities
* explain how inclusive education principles apply throughout transitions
* provide examples of questions families can ask at each stage
* encourage proactive planning before transitions occur
* present information in a clear, stage-based format

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow/ Conversation**

**Parent:**
My daughter is moving to middle school next year. What should I be paying attention to?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the transition.**
   "Transitions are important because decisions made during these periods can influence future educational opportunities and supports."
2. **Explain the concept.**
   "The focus of advocacy often changes as students move through the education system."
3. **Provide a stage-based overview.**
   * **Elementary → Middle School**
     * maintaining inclusion
     * preserving friendships and belonging
     * support continuity
     * maintaining learning expectations
   * **Middle School → High School**
     * course selection
     * educational pathways
     * graduation planning
     * future opportunities
   * **High School → Post-Secondary or Community**
     * employment preparation
     * post-secondary options
     * adult services
     * community participation
4. **Connect to inclusion.**
   "At every stage, families can consider how decisions support participation, belonging, learning, and future opportunities."
5. **Suggest questions.**
   "What decisions are being made now that could affect future opportunities?"
6. **Reinforce planning.**
   "The earlier families understand upcoming decisions, the more options they often have available."
7. **Suggest next step.**
   "Let's look at the transition your child is approaching and identify the key areas to focus on."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* transition planning frameworks
* common educational transition stages
* inclusive education principles
* stage-specific planning considerations
* educational pathway information
* graduation pathway information
* support-planning practices
* post-secondary and community transition resources
* examples of transition-planning questions
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small-Medium (1-2 days)
* This is largely a navigation and orientation story.
* The value comes from helping parents understand the transition landscape before they need to make important decisions.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* explains transition planning clearly
* identifies key stages and decision points
* connects transitions to future opportunities
* reinforces inclusive education principles
* provides useful planning questions
* presents information in an organized, stage-based manner

The system should consistently help parents answer:
*"What should I be paying attention to at this stage of my child's educational journey?"*
rather than:
*"What should I do right now?"*

## USER STORY 232: As a parent, I want to know what the expectations of my child should be at each stage so that their education is realized.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is moving through the education system and the parent wants to understand what a meaningful, age-appropriate, and inclusive educational experience should look like at each stage. They want to ensure their child is receiving appropriate learning opportunities, supports, and experiences that prepare them for future transitions and adult life.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain what educational growth and development may look like at different stages
* help parents understand common learning, participation, and transition goals associated with each stage
* explain how expectations can evolve as students move through elementary, middle, and high school
* emphasize strengths, growth, and future opportunities rather than deficits
* help parents distinguish between educational expectations and support needs
* provide examples of learning, social, community, and independence-related outcomes
* explain how inclusive education supports meaningful participation at every stage
* encourage long-term planning while recognizing individual differences

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow/ Conversation**

**Parent:**
My son is moving into high school. What should we be focusing on at this stage?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the question.**
   "Many families find it helpful to understand how priorities and opportunities evolve throughout a student's educational journey."
2. **Explain the concept.**
   "Each stage of education brings different opportunities for learning, growth, independence, participation, and future planning."
3. **Provide stage-based examples.**
   * **Elementary School**
     * foundational learning
     * friendships and belonging
     * communication and participation
     * building confidence and interests
   * **Middle School**
     * increasing independence
     * self-advocacy
     * exploration of strengths and interests
     * preparing for future educational choices
   * **High School**
     * course planning
     * employment exploration
     * graduation pathways
     * community participation
     * transition to adulthood
4. **Reinforce inclusion.**
   "At every stage, students should have opportunities to learn, participate, develop relationships, and prepare for future possibilities."
5. **Suggest reflection questions.**
   "What skills, experiences, and opportunities will help my child move successfully into the next stage?"
6. **Connect to future planning.**
   "Understanding what typically happens at each stage can help families prepare before important decisions are made."
7. **Suggest next step.**
   "Let's look at the stage your child is entering and identify the opportunities that may be most important to consider."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* educational development frameworks
* transition-planning principles
* stage-based educational expectations
* inclusive education principles
* self-advocacy and independence-building concepts
* employment and community participation planning
* social and emotional development considerations
* graduation and pathway planning information
* examples of age-appropriate opportunities
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small (1 day)
* This is primarily an educational planning and orientation story.
* The value comes from helping families understand the purpose and opportunities associated with each stage of education.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* explains stage-based expectations clearly
* focuses on opportunities and growth
* reinforces inclusive education principles
* provides age-appropriate examples
* avoids deficit-based assumptions
* encourages future planning
* recognizes that students progress differently

The system should consistently help parents answer:
*"What should a meaningful educational experience look like at this stage?"*
rather than:
*"Is my child meeting expectations?"*

## USER STORY 234: As a parent, I want to understand when (timelines, lead time) I should be having transition planning meetings with the old and new educators involved in the transition so that my child has a smooth transition.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is approaching a transition to a new grade, school, or educational environment. The parent wants to ensure that transition planning begins early enough and includes the right people so that important information, supports, expectations, and opportunities are not lost during the transition.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain the purpose of transition planning meetings
* explain why early planning is important
* identify common transition-planning timelines
* help parents understand who should typically be involved in transition planning
* explain the roles of current educators, future educators, families, and students
* identify common topics discussed during transition meetings
* encourage planning before key decisions have already been made
* explain how transition planning supports continuity of learning, supports, and inclusion
* present information in a timeline-based format

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow/ Conversation**

**Parent:**
My daughter is moving to middle school next year. When should transition planning meetings start?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the importance.**
   "Transition planning is often most effective when it begins before the transition year is complete."
2. **Explain the purpose.**
   "The goal is to ensure that information, supports, learning goals, and expectations move with the student."
3. **Explain timing.**
   "Many families benefit from beginning transition conversations months before the actual transition occurs, rather than waiting until the final weeks of the school year."
4. **Identify participants.**
   "Transition planning often involves:
   * the family
   * current educators
   * future educators
   * support staff
   * the student, when appropriate"
5. **Identify discussion topics.**
   "Meetings may include discussions about:
   * strengths and interests
   * learning goals
   * accommodations and supports
   * social inclusion
   * transportation
   * future educational pathways"
6. **Reinforce continuity.**
   "Early planning helps reduce the risk of supports, expectations, and opportunities being lost during transitions."
7. **Suggest next step.**
   "Let's identify the transition your child is approaching and discuss what planning conversations may be helpful."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* transition-planning frameworks
* common educational transition timelines
* roles and responsibilities during transitions
* transition meeting best practices
* inclusive education principles
* support continuity concepts
* examples of transition-planning agendas
* student profile and strengths-based planning resources
* family engagement practices
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small (1 day)
* This is primarily a planning and navigation story.
* The value comes from helping families understand that transition planning is a process, not a single meeting.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* explains transition planning clearly
* emphasizes early planning
* identifies key participants
* provides practical examples of discussion topics
* connects planning to successful transitions
* presents information chronologically
* reinforces continuity of supports and expectations

The system should consistently help parents answer:
*"When should transition planning begin and who should be involved?"*
rather than:
*"When is the transition meeting?"*

## USER STORY 235: As a parent, I want to know what questions to ask so that my child's transition is successful.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent is preparing for a transition planning meeting and wants to ensure important topics are discussed before decisions are made. They are unsure what questions to ask and want guidance that will help identify opportunities, risks, supports, and future planning considerations.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain the purpose of asking questions during transition planning
* provide examples of important questions based on the transition stage
* help parents identify areas that are commonly overlooked
* organize questions into meaningful categories
* explain why each category matters
* encourage discussions about learning, inclusion, supports, friendships, future opportunities, and student goals
* help parents personalize questions to their child's situation
* present information in an easy-to-use checklist format

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**

**Parent:**
We have a transition meeting next month. What should I be asking?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.**
   "Preparing questions in advance can help ensure important topics are discussed before decisions are finalized."
2. **Organize the discussion.**
   * **Learning and Educational Goals**
     * What learning goals are being prioritized?
     * How will progress be measured?
     * How will expectations be maintained?
   * **Supports and Accommodations**
     * What supports will continue?
     * What changes should we expect?
     * How will successful supports transfer to the new setting?
   * **Inclusion and Participation**
     * How will my child participate with peers?
     * What extracurricular opportunities will be available?
     * How will belonging and friendships be supported?
   * **Future Planning**
     * How does this transition affect future opportunities?
     * What pathways remain available?
     * What decisions will need to be made next?
3. **Highlight commonly missed topics.**
   "Families often focus on supports but may forget to discuss future opportunities, course planning, or social inclusion."
4. **Encourage personalization.**
   "The most useful questions are often connected to your child's strengths, interests, goals, and aspirations."
5. **Reinforce preparation.**
   "Having questions prepared can help families participate more confidently and ensure important topics are not overlooked."
6. **Suggest next step.**
   "Let's build a personalized question list based on your child's upcoming transition."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* transition-planning frameworks
* transition meeting best practices
* stage-specific transition considerations
* inclusive education principles
* support and accommodation planning resources
* course and pathway planning information
* friendship and belonging concepts
* post-secondary and community transition resources
* examples of successful transition planning discussions
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small (1 day)
* This is primarily a planning and preparation story.
* The value comes from helping parents arrive at meetings informed and ready to participate.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* generates relevant questions
* organizes questions logically
* includes both educational and social considerations
* identifies commonly overlooked topics
* adapts questions to different transition stages
* encourages future-focused planning

The system should consistently help parents answer:
*"What questions should I ask to ensure nothing important is missed?"*
rather than:
*"What should I say at the meeting?"*

## USER STORY 237: As a parent, I want to know what the challenges/resistance are at each stage of so that I can ensure I properly advocate.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is approaching a major educational transition and the parent wants to understand the common challenges, barriers, and points of resistance that families often encounter. They want to prepare in advance so they can identify concerns early and advocate effectively before opportunities are lost.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* identify common transition-related challenges at each educational stage
* explain why these challenges often occur
* help parents recognize early warning signs
* explain how challenges may affect inclusion, learning, supports, and future opportunities
* provide examples of questions families can ask when concerns arise
* help parents prepare proactively rather than reactively
* present information in a stage-based format
* avoid assuming every transition will encounter difficulties

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**

**Parent:**
My son is moving to high school next year. What should I be watching out for?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.**
   "Every transition is different, but families often encounter similar challenges at key transition points."
2. **Explain the purpose.**
   "Understanding common challenges can help families identify concerns early and participate proactively in planning."
3. **Provide a stage-based overview.**
   * **Elementary → Middle School**
     Common challenges may include:
     * changes in support structures
     * loss of continuity between educators
     * difficulty maintaining friendships
     * reduced familiarity with student strengths and needs
   * **Middle School → High School**
     Common challenges may include:
     * pathway and course-selection decisions
     * reduced educational expectations
     * pressure toward segregated or alternate programming
     * graduation planning concerns
   * **High School → Post-Secondary / Community**
     Common challenges may include:
     * service transitions
     * employment preparation gaps
     * reduced support availability
     * uncertainty regarding future pathways
4. **Identify warning signs.**
   "Families may wish to pay attention when important decisions are being made without discussion, when expectations begin to decrease, or when future opportunities are being limited without clear rationale."
5. **Suggest questions.**
   "You may wish to ask:
   * What risks are associated with this transition?
   * What barriers have been identified?
   * How will opportunities be preserved?
   * What planning is occurring to address these concerns?"
6. **Reinforce preparedness.**
   "Most challenges are easier to address when they are anticipated rather than discovered after decisions have already been made."
7. **Suggest next step.**
   "Let's explore the transition your child is approaching and identify the challenges families commonly encounter."

* common transition challenges by stage

* educational pathway decision points
* support continuity concerns
* graduation and transition planning information

* employment and adult-life transition concepts
* examples of successful transition planning practices
* warning signs and risk indicators
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

## USER STORY 238: As a parent, I want to know what the roadblocks that I might face so that I can be prepared to ask the right questions.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent is preparing for an educational transition and wants to understand the practical obstacles, barriers, and process-related issues that families commonly encounter. They want to anticipate these roadblocks so they can ask informed questions, participate effectively, and avoid being caught off guard by decisions or delays.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* identify common process-related roadblocks families encounter during transitions
* explain how those roadblocks can affect educational opportunities and planning
* help parents recognize early warning signs
* provide examples of questions that can help uncover potential issues
* explain strategies families commonly use to address roadblocks
* distinguish between normal transition activities and situations that may require follow-up
* encourage proactive participation in planning
* present information in a practical, family-friendly format

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**

**Parent:**
What kinds of obstacles should I expect during this transition?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.**
   "Many families encounter similar challenges during transitions. Knowing what to look for can help you identify concerns early."
2. **Explain the purpose.**
   "Roadblocks are not always intentional. They often result from communication gaps, unclear responsibilities, competing priorities, or lack of planning."
3. **Provide examples.**
   **Common Transition Roadblocks**
   * transition planning starts too late
   * important stakeholders are not involved
   * information is not shared between schools
   * expectations are lowered without discussion
   * pathway decisions are made before alternatives are explored
   * support discussions focus only on logistics rather than future opportunities
   * families are unaware of important timelines
   * responsibilities are unclear between current and future educators
4. **Identify questions.**
   "You may wish to ask:
   * Who is responsible for coordinating this transition?
   * Who needs to be involved?
   * What decisions will be made and when?
   * What information is being shared?
   * What opportunities might be affected by these decisions?"
5. **Explain warning signs.**
   "If decisions appear to be occurring without discussion, if planning is delayed, or if future opportunities are not being considered, additional questions may be helpful."
6. **Reinforce preparation.**
   "Most roadblocks are easier to address when they are identified early rather than after decisions have already been made."
7. **Suggest next step.**
   "Let's look at your upcoming transition and identify the roadblocks families commonly encounter."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* transition-planning frameworks
* common transition barriers and failure points
* communication and coordination challenges
* educational pathway decision points
* inclusive education principles
* transition meeting practices
* roles and responsibilities during transitions
* examples of successful transition planning
* warning signs and risk indicators
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small (1 day)
* This is primarily a planning and preparation story.
* The value comes from helping families identify system-level obstacles before those obstacles affect opportunities.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* identifies realistic roadblocks
* distinguishes roadblocks from educational challenges
* provides practical questions families can ask
* encourages proactive planning
* explains warning signs clearly

* focuses on preparation and navigation

*"What obstacles might prevent this transition from going smoothly?"*

*"Who is causing the problem?"*
