## USER STORY 131: As a parent, I want to know what rights my child has so that my child gets
an education.
### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent is trying to make decisions and advocate for their child within the education system, but is
unsure what rights their child has regarding access to education, participation, accommodations, and
support. Without understanding these rights, the parent struggles to determine whether what is
happening is appropriate or whether additional advocacy may be needed.
### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
explain educational rights in plain language
help parents understand the relationship between rights and inclusive education
identify common rights related to access, participation, accommodations, and support
explain that rights may be protected through legislation, policies, and international frameworks
provide examples of how rights apply in everyday school situations
distinguish between rights, supports, and educational decisions
provide references to trusted sources and policies
encourage informed advocacy based on understanding rather than confrontation
avoid providing legal advice or jurisdiction-specific legal interpretations
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow/Conversation**
Parent:
I don't even know what rights my child has. How do I know if what the school is doing is okay?
System:
1. Acknowledge the concern.
"Many parents have the same question. Understanding your child's rights can help you ask
informed questions and participate confidently in educational decisions."
2. Explain the concept.
"Educational rights help ensure students have access to learning opportunities, participation,
supports, and accommodations that allow them to benefit from education."
3. Provide examples.
"Rights-related topics may include:
- access to education
- participation alongside peers
- reasonable accommodations and supports
- involvement in educational planning
- protection from discrimination"
4. Connect to inclusion.
"Inclusive education principles help guide how these rights are applied in practice."
5. Explain practical value.
"Understanding rights can help families ask questions, seek clarification, and advocate
effectively when concerns arise."
6. Reinforce confidence.
"Knowing your child's rights does not mean you need to become a legal expert. It simply helps
you participate more confidently in discussions and decisions."
7. Suggest next step.
"Let's explore which rights may be most relevant to your child's situation."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
plain-language explanations of educational rights
inclusive education principles
participation and accommodation rights
accessibility and support concepts
relevant legislation and policy frameworks
CRPD Article 24 summaries
provincial and territorial education policy references
examples of rights in practice
common misconceptions about educational rights
trusted reference sources
**Notes**
Estimated Complexity
Small-Medium (1-2 days)
The complexity lies in presenting rights accurately while keeping the information understandable and
avoiding legal interpretation.
**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
explains rights in plain language
provides practical examples
connects rights to real educational situations
references credible sources
avoids legal advice
reinforces informed advocacy rather than confrontation
The system should consistently help parents answer:
"What protections and opportunities should my child reasonably expect within the education system?"
rather than:
"How do I win an argument with the school?"

## USER STORY 134: As a parent, I want to know what the bar for undue hardship is so that I know if
the school has genuinely reached that level.
### 1. What's the situation here?
A parent has been told that a support, accommodation, or inclusive educational option cannot be
provided due to limitations faced by the school or education system. The parent wants to understand
what "undue hardship" means and how to determine whether all reasonable options have genuinely
been explored before participation is restricted.
### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
explain undue hardship in plain language
explain why the concept exists within human rights frameworks
describe common factors that may be considered when assessing undue hardship
explain that undue hardship is generally intended to be a high threshold
help parents understand that inconvenience and undue hardship are not necessarily the same
thing
provide examples to illustrate the concept
encourage parents to ask questions about options that were considered
explain that determinations may vary depending on circumstances and jurisdiction
avoid making legal determinations or conclusions
### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**
Parent:
The school says they can't support my daughter on field trips because it would create undue hardship.
What does that actually mean?
System:
1. Acknowledge the question.
"Undue hardship is an important concept in human rights and inclusive education discussions,
but it can sometimes be difficult to understand."
2. Define the concept.
"In simple terms, undue hardship refers to situations where providing an accommodation would
create a significant burden that goes beyond what an organization is reasonably expected to
provide."
3. Explain the threshold.
"The concept is generally intended to represent a high threshold. It is typically more than
inconvenience, preference, or administrative difficulty."
4. Explain the purpose.
"The goal is to encourage organizations to explore reasonable accommodations and supports
before limiting participation."
5. Encourage questions.
"Parents may wish to ask:
- What options were considered?
- What supports were explored?
- Why were those options not feasible?
- What alternatives were discussed?"
6. Clarify limitations.
"Whether undue hardship has been reached depends on specific facts and circumstances and
may require further review."
7. Suggest next step.
"Let's explore how this concept relates to your child's situation and what information may help
you better understand the decision."
### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Review Required**
The system needs:
plain-language explanations of undue hardship
human rights accommodation principles
inclusive education and participation concepts
examples of accommodation discussions
common misconceptions about undue hardship
distinction between inconvenience and undue hardship
examples of questions families can ask
