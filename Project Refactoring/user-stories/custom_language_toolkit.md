# Use this, not that (Inclusion Canada)

| Instead of... | Please use... |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Birth defect, congenital defect, deformity** | Person born with a disability |
| **Blind, visually impaired, 'the Blind' (as a collective noun)** | Person who is blind; person who is partially sighted; person with a vision disability; person with sight loss; person with vision loss; people who are blind<br><br>*\*Note: People who have some degree of vision may refer to themselves as blind, or partially sighted.* |
| **Canadians, Citizens** | People in Canada<br><br>*\*Note: Many Indigenous people do not consider themselves Canadian. Many im/migrants and newcomers are not citizens of Canada.* |
| **Care** | Support<br><br>*\*Note: In the context of attendant care which is a specific type of support - the term "care" is considered appropriate.* |
| **Caregiver** | Support person; paid support person; staff person |
| **Committed suicide** | Died by suicide; ended their life; lost their life to suicide |
| **Confined to, restricted to, dependent on, wheelchair-bound** | Person who uses a wheelchair |
| **Cripple, crippled, lame** | Person with a mobility disability<br><br>*\*Note: 'Person with a mobility disability' is a general description that may refer to one or several specific disabilities. If relevant, you can specify the type of disability, for example, 'person who has a spinal cord injury' or 'person who has quadriplegia'.* |
| **Deaf-mute, deaf and dumb** | Person who is deaf; deaf person; Deaf person<br><br>*\*Note: The Deaf community is a distinct community both culturally and linguistically. People who are of this community do not identify themselves as having a disability but rather a different first language - Sign Language. Capitalize "Deaf" when you are talking about the community or cultural identity, rather than the experience of becoming deaf. Generally, people who refer to themselves as Deaf use Sign Language and identify as part of the Deaf community. Often, they were born deaf or acquired deafness at a young age. People who refer to themselves as 'deaf' may not identify themselves as a member of the Deaf community, may have had a bilingual education, may not use Sign Language, and may be born deaf or acquire deafness later in life. To encompass both communities, use 'people who are d/Deaf'. Individual preferences vary. This is distinct from people who are hard of hearing.* |
| **Deformed** | Person with a facial difference; person with a limb difference |
| **Differently abled** | Person with a disability |
| **Downs, Down's Syndrome** | Person with Down syndrome; person who has Down syndrome |
| **Epileptic** | Person who has epilepsy |
| **Fit, attack, spell** | Seizure |
| **Handicapped, Disabled, 'the disabled'** | Person with a disability; people with disabilities |
| **Handicapped parking, disabled bathrooms** | Accessible parking; accessible bathrooms |
| **Hearing impaired** | Person who is hard of hearing<br><br>*\*Note: This group of people are not d/Deaf. Due to hearing loss, they may use amplification devices. The term 'hearing impaired' is viewed as negative. It also fails to recognize the difference between the Deaf and hard of hearing community.* |
| **He/She** | They |
| **High-functioning autistic, low-functioning autistic** | Person on the Autism spectrum; Autistic person<br><br>*\*Note: 'High' and 'low' functioning labels and categorization are dehumanizing regardless of disability. People with Autism have diverse opinions on language. Many prefer identity-first language. If you are unsure, ask the person.* |
| **Inarticulate, incoherent** | Person with a communication disability; person with a speech disability |
| **Incompetent, mentally incompetent** | Person under a guardianship order<br><br>*\*Note: 'Mentally incompetent' as a legal term is still used in some jurisdictions. This term should be used only in a legal context.* |
| **Individuals with disabilities** | People with disabilities<br><br>*\*Note: Many self-advocates have expressed a dislike for the term 'individuals' because it feels impersonal and dehumanizing and is often used only in the context of people with disabilities. Inclusion Canada uses 'people' or 'person' as the general rule.* |
| **Intellectually disabled** | Person with an intellectual disability; people with an intellectual disability |
| **Invalid** | Person with a disability |
| **Learning disabled, learning disordered, dyslexic, learning difficulty** | Person with a learning disability<br><br>*\*Note: 'Person with a learning disability' is a general description that may refer to one or several specific disabilities. If relevant, you can specify the type of disability, for example, 'person who has dyslexia.'* |
| **Living with a disability** | Person with a disability<br><br>*\*Note: The phrase 'living with' implies that a disability is a negative burden. Additionally, 'living with' a disability does not accurately describe disabilities that are episodic. If the nature of the disability is relevant, use terms like 'episodic', 'progressive', 'recurrent', 'fluctuating', or 'continuous'.* |
| **Mentally ill, insane, unsound mind, lunatic, maniac, mental patient, mentally diseased, neurotic, psychotic, mental, crazy** | Person with a psychosocial disability; person with mental illness<br><br>*\*Note: 'Unsound mind' as a legal term is still used in some jurisdictions. This term should be used only when in a legal context.*<br><br>*\*Additional Note: The terms 'person with mental illness' and 'psychosocial disabilities' are broad. If relevant, you can specify the type of disability, for example, 'person who has depression' or 'person who has schizophrenia.'* |
| **Mentally retarded, retarded, mentally challenged, developmentally delayed** | Person with an intellectual disability; person with a developmental disability; person with a cognitive disability |
| **Midget; Dwarf** | Little person; person of short stature |
| **Non-verbal, non-communicative** | Person who does not communicate with words or sign; person who communicates using (a communication device, gestures, etc.) |
| **Normal, able-bodied, non-disabled person** | Person without a disability |
| **Physically challenged, physically handicapped, physically impaired** | Person with a physical disability |
| **Placed, placement (in the context of employment or housing), "She has a job placement", "They were placed in a special care home"** | Person who is employed; person who is not employed; person living at (a special care home)<br><br>*\*Note: Where applicable in the context of employment, refer to someone by their career identity ("She is a tradesperson") or job title (They are a lab technician)."* |
| **Profoundly disabled, severe disability, referring to the person using their supports, "He's a level 4"** | Person with a significant disability |
| **Sign language** | Sign Language (all languages are capitalized in English)<br><br>*\*Note: Where applicable, refer to the specific language: American Sign Language (ASL), Langue des signes Québécoise (LSQ), etc. Indigenous sign language is capitalized only when referring to a specific Indigenous sign language. Black American Sign Language (BASL) is a dialect that differs from ASL in several ways: larger signing space; signing location; two hand variants; and influence of African American English.* |
| **Son, daughter, sister, brother, mother, father (when speaking generally)** | Child(ren); family member; sibling; parent(s) |
| **Spastic** | Person who has spasms |
| **Special Needs people, Person with special needs, People that have special needs** | People with a disability; person with a disability who requires (specific supports) |
| **Suffers from, stricken with, afflicted by** | They have...; a person with...<br><br>*\*Note: People with disabilities do not necessarily suffer.* |
| **Those people, Special people** | People |
| **Victim of** | Person with (cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, etc.)<br><br>*\*Note: 'Victim' as a legal term is still used widely in the context of criminal actions. Outside of legal settings, some people who have experienced violent crime may prefer use of the word 'survivor'. If you are unsure, ask the person.* |
| **Women and girls (in the context of gender-based violence)** | Women, girls, and gender-diverse people |
