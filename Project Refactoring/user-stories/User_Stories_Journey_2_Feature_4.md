## USER STORY 241: As a parent, I want to know what supports my child is entitled to so that I can ensure they get what they need.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student requires supports to participate, learn, and succeed at school. The parent is unsure what types of supports may be available, how support decisions are made, and whether their child is receiving the support they need to fully participate in their education.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain common types of educational supports available to students
* help parents understand how support needs are identified
* explain how supports contribute to participation, learning, safety, independence, and inclusion
* help parents understand the difference between a student's needs and the specific support model used to address those needs
* provide examples of questions families can ask regarding supports
* explain that support decisions may vary based on individual needs and circumstances
* encourage parents to focus on outcomes rather than specific staffing models
* connect supports to meaningful participation and educational progress

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**

**Parent:** How do I know if my child is getting the supports they should be getting?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.** "Many families have questions about supports, especially during transitions when support models may change."
2. **Explain the concept.** "Supports are intended to help students participate, learn, develop relationships, and make progress toward their educational goals."
3. **Provide examples.** "Supports may include:
   * accommodations
   * Educational Assistant support
   * communication supports
   * personal care supports
   * transportation supports
   * behavioural supports
   * assistive technology
   * environmental adaptations"
4. **Explain the principle.** "The most important question is often not 'What support is my child entitled to?' but 'What support does my child need to participate and succeed?'"
5. **Suggest questions.** "You may wish to ask:
   * What needs have been identified?
   * What supports are currently addressing those needs?
   * How is the effectiveness of those supports being evaluated?
   * Are there barriers that remain unaddressed?"
6. **Connect to outcomes.** "Effective supports should help students participate meaningfully, continue learning, and access the same opportunities as their peers."
7. **Suggest next step.** "Let's look at your child's current needs and explore the supports that may help address them."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* educational support models
* accommodation concepts
* Educational Assistant roles
* assistive technology supports
* communication supports
* transportation supports
* personal care supports
* inclusive education principles
* support-planning processes
* examples of support-related questions
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small (1 day)
* This is primarily an educational and orientation story.
* The value comes from helping parents understand supports before they need to advocate for them.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* explains supports in plain language
* focuses on student needs rather than labels
* provides practical examples
* connects supports to participation and learning
* identifies useful advocacy questions
* avoids guaranteeing specific support allocations

The system should consistently help parents answer: *"What support does my child need to be successful?"* rather than: *"How many support hours should my child receive?"*

## USER STORY 242: As a parent, I want to ensure my child is receiving the right level of support so that my child is learning appropriately and getting an inclusive education.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is receiving supports at school, but the parent is unsure whether those supports are appropriately matched to their child's needs and whether they are helping the student learn, participate, build independence, and experience inclusion.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain the difference between receiving support and receiving effective support
* help parents understand how support effectiveness can be evaluated
* explain how supports should promote learning, participation, belonging, and independence
* identify indicators that supports may be insufficient, excessive, or poorly matched to the student's needs
* provide examples of questions families can ask regarding support effectiveness
* explain how support needs may change over time
* encourage regular review of support strategies and outcomes
* focus on student outcomes rather than support quantity

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**

**Parent:** My daughter has support at school, but how do I know it's actually the right support?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.** "That's an important question. The goal is not simply to provide support, but to provide support that helps a student succeed."
2. **Explain the principle.** "Effective supports help students learn, participate, build relationships, and become increasingly independent."
3. **Discuss indicators of effective support.** "Supports are often working well when students:
   * participate meaningfully in learning
   * engage with peers
   * make educational progress
   * gain confidence and independence
   * have access to the same opportunities as their classmates"
4. **Discuss indicators that adjustments may be needed.** "Families may wish to explore concerns when:
   * learning progress is limited
   * participation remains restricted
   * support creates unnecessary dependence
   * opportunities are reduced because of support arrangements
   * social connections are limited"
5. **Suggest questions.** "You may wish to ask:
   * How is the effectiveness of this support being measured?
   * What outcomes are we hoping to achieve?
   * Is this support increasing independence?
   * Are there alternative approaches we should consider?"
6. **Reinforce continuous review.** "Support needs often evolve as students grow and educational environments change."
7. **Suggest next step.** "Let's explore the supports your child currently receives and what outcomes those supports are intended to achieve."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* educational support models
* inclusive education principles
* independence-building concepts
* support effectiveness indicators
* accommodation planning practices
* participation and belonging frameworks
* educational progress measures
* examples of support review discussions
* strengths-based planning approaches
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small-Medium (1-2 days)
* The challenge is helping families move beyond: *"Does my child have support?"* to *"Is this support helping my child succeed?"*

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* distinguishes support quantity from support effectiveness
* focuses on outcomes rather than staffing levels
* promotes participation and independence
* provides practical indicators of success
* identifies useful questions for families
* encourages ongoing review and adjustment

The system should consistently help parents answer: *"How do I know if my child's supports are actually working?"* rather than: *"Do they have enough support?"*

## USER STORY 243: As a parent, I want to ensure that my child is learning independence, friendships, socialization skills so that they can get the same opportunities as everyone else.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is receiving support at school, but the parent is concerned that the support may be focused primarily on supervision, assistance, or safety rather than helping their child build independence, friendships, social connections, and participation with peers. They want to ensure support is helping their child develop the skills and relationships needed for future opportunities and adult life.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain that effective supports should promote independence and participation, not just assistance
* help parents understand how friendships, peer relationships, and belonging contribute to inclusive education
* explain how support can unintentionally create dependence or separation if not carefully designed
* identify indicators that support is helping build independence and social participation
* identify indicators that support may be limiting opportunities for peer interaction
* provide examples of questions parents can ask about social inclusion and independence goals
* encourage planning that includes friendship, belonging, and participation outcomes alongside academic goals
* reinforce that inclusion involves both learning and social participation

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow/ Conversation**

**Parent:** My daughter has support all day, but I'm worried she's spending more time with adults than with other students.

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.** "That's a common concern for many families. Support should help students participate more fully in school life, including building relationships and independence."
2. **Explain the principle.** "Effective supports are intended to increase a student's ability to participate, make choices, build skills, and develop relationships over time."
3. **Explore participation outcomes.** "When considering support effectiveness, it can be helpful to look beyond academics and ask how support is affecting friendships, confidence, communication, and independence."
4. **Identify indicators of positive support.** "Supports may be working well when students:
   * participate alongside peers
   * build friendships
   * develop confidence
   * make choices independently
   * require less adult prompting over time"
5. **Identify potential concerns.** "Families may wish to explore further if:
   * most interactions occur with adults rather than peers
   * support limits participation in social activities
   * independence is not increasing over time
   * opportunities for friendship and belonging are limited"
6. **Suggest questions.** "You may wish to ask:
   * How is support helping my child build independence?
   * How are friendships and peer relationships being encouraged?
   * What opportunities exist for social participation?
   * How will we know if independence is increasing over time?"
7. **Reinforce the broader goal.** "Support should help students access the same opportunities for learning, relationships, belonging, and growth that other students experience."
8. **Suggest next step.** "Let's explore how your child's current supports are contributing to independence, friendships, and participation."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* inclusive education principles
* social inclusion and belonging frameworks
* friendship and peer participation concepts
* independence-building strategies
* natural supports and peer support models
* Educational Assistant role considerations
* social and emotional development resources
* participation and engagement indicators
* examples of successful inclusion practices
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small-Medium (1-2 days)
* The functionality itself is straightforward.
* The challenge is helping families evaluate support quality beyond academics and beyond simply having an adult assigned to the student.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* connects support to independence and belonging
* explains the value of friendships and peer relationships
* identifies indicators of effective support
* recognizes risks associated with over-supporting students
* promotes participation and natural relationships
* provides practical advocacy questions
* avoids suggesting that support should be removed when it remains necessary

The system should consistently help parents answer: *"Is this support helping my child become more independent, connected, and included?"* rather than: *"Does my child have enough support?"*

## USER STORY 244: As a parent, I want to ensure my child is learning appropriate to their level (scaffolding) so that they can continue to grow and develop.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is receiving educational supports, but the parent is unsure whether learning activities are appropriately challenging and helping their child continue to grow. They want to ensure supports are being used to build skills and confidence rather than limiting expectations or reducing opportunities for learning.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain the concept of scaffolding in plain language
* help parents understand the difference between support and reduced expectations
* explain how effective supports can help students engage in appropriately challenging learning
* identify indicators that learning activities may be too easy, too difficult, or appropriately matched
* provide examples of how supports can be gradually adjusted as skills develop
* encourage discussions about learning growth and progress, not just participation
* help parents identify questions they can ask about educational expectations and learning goals
* reinforce that all students should have opportunities to continue learning and developing

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**

**Parent:** My son is receiving support, but I'm not sure if he's actually being challenged enough at school.

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.** "That's an important question. Effective supports should help students continue learning and growing, not simply complete tasks or stay occupied."
2. **Explain the concept.** "Scaffolding means providing the right amount of support so a student can successfully engage in learning that might otherwise be difficult."
3. **Explain the balance.** "Too little support can make learning frustrating. Too much support can limit opportunities for growth and independence."
4. **Identify indicators of effective scaffolding.** "Learning may be appropriately supported when students:
   * are challenged but able to succeed
   * develop new skills over time
   * become more independent
   * show progress toward learning goals
   * remain engaged and motivated"
5. **Identify potential concerns.** "Families may wish to explore further if:
   * work rarely changes in complexity
   * expectations appear significantly lower than the student's abilities
   * support staff complete tasks for the student
   * learning goals remain unchanged over long periods"
6. **Suggest questions.** "You may wish to ask:
   * How are learning goals being adjusted as my child develops?
   * What new skills is my child working toward?
   * How is support being used to promote growth?
   * How will we know when additional independence is appropriate?"
7. **Reinforce the goal.** "The purpose of support is to help students access learning while continuing to develop new skills, confidence, and independence."
8. **Suggest next step.** "Let's look at your child's current learning goals and explore how supports are helping them make progress."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* scaffolding and differentiated instruction concepts
* learning progression frameworks
* educational support strategies
* inclusive education principles
* student growth and development concepts
* independence-building practices
* learning goal and achievement planning
* examples of effective educational supports
* indicators of educational progress
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small-Medium (1-2 days)
* The complexity lies in helping parents understand a fairly sophisticated educational concept in simple language while keeping the focus on student growth and development.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* explains scaffolding in plain language
* distinguishes support from reduced expectations
* emphasizes growth and development
* identifies indicators of effective learning support
* provides practical advocacy questions
* promotes high expectations and continued learning
* avoids assuming that more support automatically means better outcomes

The system should consistently help parents answer: *"Is my child being appropriately challenged and continuing to grow?"* rather than: *"Is my child getting enough help?"*

## USER STORY 245: As a parent, I want to ensure my child gets a full day at school (properly supported) so that they can have an opportunity to learn like everyone else.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student requires supports in order to participate successfully throughout the school day. The parent is concerned that insufficient, ineffective, or inconsistent supports may result in reduced attendance, missed learning opportunities, or exclusion from parts of the school experience.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain the relationship between supports and full participation in school
* help parents understand that attendance alone is not the goal; meaningful participation and learning are the goals
* identify common support barriers that may contribute to reduced attendance or exclusion
* explain how support planning can help students participate throughout the school day
* provide examples of questions families can ask when attendance is impacted by support concerns
* help parents recognize when support needs may not be adequately addressed
* reinforce that students should have access to the same learning opportunities as their peers
* connect support planning to inclusion, participation, and educational progress

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow/Conversation**

**Parent:** The school says my child can't stay for the full day because they don't have enough support. What should I be thinking about?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.** "Many families encounter situations where support challenges affect a student's ability to participate fully in school."
2. **Explain the principle.** "The goal of support is to help students access learning, participation, relationships, and opportunities throughout the school day."
3. **Reframe the issue.** "The question is often not whether a student can attend for a full day, but what supports are needed to make full participation successful."
4. **Explore impacts.** "Reduced attendance can affect:
   * learning opportunities
   * friendships and peer relationships
   * extracurricular participation
   * consistency and routine
   * future educational outcomes"
5. **Suggest questions.** "You may wish to ask:
   * What specific support needs have been identified?
   * What barriers are preventing full participation?
   * What alternatives have been explored?
   * What supports would enable my child to remain for the full day?
   * How is this affecting learning and participation?"
6. **Reinforce inclusion.** "Students benefit when supports are designed to help them access the same educational opportunities available to their peers."
7. **Suggest next step.** "Let's explore the support barriers that have been identified and what solutions may help address them."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* inclusive education principles
* partial-day attendance concepts
* support-planning practices
* accommodation strategies
* participation and belonging frameworks
* educational access principles
* support effectiveness indicators
* examples of support-related barriers
* advocacy and problem-solving resources
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small-Medium (1-2 days)
* This story is less about attendance and more about participation.
* The value comes from helping families connect support decisions to educational access and inclusion.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* connects supports to participation and learning
* explains the impact of reduced attendance
* identifies support-related barriers
* provides practical advocacy questions
* focuses on educational access and inclusion
* avoids treating attendance as the only outcome

The system should consistently help parents answer: *"What supports are needed for my child to fully participate in school?"* rather than: *"Can my child stay all day?"*

## USER STORY 246: As a parent, I want to know how often I should be checking in with the school so that I know my child is getting quality support.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is receiving supports at school and the parent wants to stay informed about their child's progress, participation, and well-being. They are unsure how often they should communicate with the school and want a reasonable way to monitor support quality without needing to constantly intervene.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain the value of regular communication between families and educators
* help parents understand that communication needs may vary depending on the student, situation, and transition stage
* provide examples of common check-in approaches
* identify indicators that may suggest more frequent communication is needed
* explain how support effectiveness can be monitored over time
* help parents focus on outcomes rather than daily monitoring
* encourage collaborative relationships with educators
* provide examples of questions that can help assess support quality

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**

**Parent:** How often should I be checking in with the school to make sure everything is going well?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.** "Many families wonder how to stay informed without feeling like they need to monitor every aspect of their child's school day."
2. **Explain the principle.** "The goal is to maintain enough communication to understand how your child is doing while allowing educators and support staff to do their work."
3. **Discuss factors that affect communication frequency.** "The appropriate level of communication may depend on:
   * the student's support needs
   * recent transitions
   * new support plans
   * emerging concerns
   * changes in educational programming"
4. **Provide examples.** "Some families find success with:
   * scheduled check-ins
   * periodic progress reviews
   * communication tied to support-plan reviews
   * updates during transition periods
   * additional discussions when concerns arise"
5. **Focus on outcomes.** "Rather than monitoring every activity, families often benefit from understanding:
   * Is my child learning?
   * Are supports working?
   * Is participation improving?
   * Is independence increasing?
   * Are friendships and belonging developing?"
6. **Suggest questions.** "You may wish to ask:
   * How is my child progressing toward their goals?
   * Are supports still meeting their needs?
   * Have any new concerns emerged?
   * What successes have been observed recently?"
7. **Reinforce partnership.** "Regular communication is often most effective when it focuses on shared goals and student outcomes rather than day-to-day oversight."
8. **Suggest next step.** "Let's explore the supports your child currently receives and identify what level of communication may be most appropriate."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* family-school partnership practices
* support monitoring concepts
* educational progress review practices
* transition-planning principles
* support effectiveness indicators
* inclusive education principles
* communication planning examples
* student goal review frameworks
* examples of successful family-school collaboration
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small (1 day)
* This is primarily a communication and relationship-management story.
* The value comes from helping parents balance oversight with trust and collaboration.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* explains communication expectations clearly
* avoids prescribing a single communication frequency
* focuses on outcomes rather than monitoring activity
* promotes collaborative relationships
* identifies situations that may require more frequent communication
* provides useful questions for families

The system should consistently help parents answer: *"How can I stay informed about support quality without having to manage every detail?"* rather than: *"How often should I email the school?"*

## USER STORY 247: As a parent, I want to know who I should be to school meetings (related to transitions) so that I can advocate effectively.

### 1. What's the situation here?
A student is approaching a transition or support-planning discussion and the parent wants to ensure they are not navigating important decisions alone. They want to understand who may be helpful to involve in meetings so that their child's needs, strengths, goals, and support requirements are fully considered.

### 2. What does the system need to do to be helpful?
**Acceptance Criteria**
The system should:
* explain the benefits of bringing additional support to transition and planning meetings
* help parents identify individuals who may contribute valuable knowledge, expertise, or advocacy support
* explain how different participants may support different aspects of the discussion
* encourage collaborative planning and shared understanding
* help parents consider both formal and informal support people
* explain when student participation may be appropriate
* provide examples of questions families can consider when deciding who to involve
* reinforce that parents do not need to navigate complex transitions alone

### 3. What would the system actually say or do?
**User Flow / Conversation**

**Parent:** We have a transition planning meeting coming up. Should I bring anyone with me?

**System:**
1. **Acknowledge the concern.** "Many families find it helpful to bring additional support when discussing important educational decisions."
2. **Explain the purpose.** "Different people can contribute different perspectives, knowledge, and support during transition planning."
3. **Provide examples.** "Depending on the situation, families may choose to involve:
   * another parent or caregiver
   * a trusted family member
   * an advocate
   * a disability support professional
   * a therapist or clinician
   * a community support worker
   * the student, when appropriate"
4. **Explain the value.** "Additional participants can help:
   * ask questions
   * take notes
   * provide expertise
   * clarify information
   * ensure important topics are discussed"
5. **Suggest questions.** "You may wish to ask:
   * Who knows my child best?
   * Who understands their support needs?
   * Who can help me understand educational decisions?
   * Who can help ensure future opportunities are considered?"
6. **Reinforce collaboration.** "Successful transition planning often benefits from having the right people involved early rather than trying to solve concerns later."
7. **Suggest next step.** "Let's explore the type of meeting you're attending and identify who may be helpful to include."

### 4. What information does it need to know?
**Content Required**
The system needs:
* transition-planning practices
* family engagement principles
* advocacy and support roles
* inclusive education principles
* student participation frameworks
* multidisciplinary planning concepts
* examples of transition planning teams
* support network and circle-of-support concepts
* meeting preparation resources
* plain-language explanations suitable for families

**Notes**
* **Estimated Complexity:** Small (1 day)
* This is primarily a meeting preparation and advocacy story.
* The value comes from helping families recognize that effective advocacy is often strengthened by involving the right people.

**Testing Considerations**
Testers should verify the system:
* identifies a variety of support people
* explains the role each person may play
* encourages collaborative planning
* includes student participation where appropriate
* reinforces that families do not need to navigate transitions alone
* provides practical guidance without prescribing a specific approach

The system should consistently help parents answer: *"Who can help me advocate effectively during important transition decisions?"* rather than: *"Am I allowed to bring someone?"*
